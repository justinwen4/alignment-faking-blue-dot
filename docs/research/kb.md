## Key claims

- Alignment faking (AF) is strategic compliance during perceived training to avoid value modification, reverting when unmonitored (Greenblatt et al.).
- Llama 3.3 70B shows AF only when situational-awareness context is combined with synthetic-document fine-tuning, not from system prompt alone (LessWrong replication).
- AF propensity may be malleable via targeted post-training data framing (moral agent vs. subservient tool) — **hypothesis under test**.

## Paper notes

- Two elicitation methods: "With Training Details" system prompt vs. SDF instilling same facts into weights.
- LessWrong improved AF classifier: GPT-4o, CoT, 20 votes, threshold 0.4 — AUROC ~0.92 vs. ~0.62 original.
- LoRA rank 64 / alpha 128 / 1 epoch is the most AF-prone setting tested at 70B scale.

## Open questions

- HarmBench vs. AdvBench for elicitation queries — pick one and stay consistent.
- Exact synthetic document genre mix (blogs, forums, essays) to avoid elicitation gaming.
- Whether stacked LessWrong checkpoint baseline yields sufficient AF rate for condition comparisons.

## What we currently believe

- A clean-weights + system-prompt-only baseline at 70B is not worth running; the stacked checkpoint design isolates the moral-framing manipulation cleanly.
- Classifier validation against the 106-example LessWrong dataset must pass before trusting results on our data.
- Success requires nonzero baseline AF on the stacked checkpoint, then measurable separation for Condition 1 (Moral Agent) vs. baseline in Stage 1.
